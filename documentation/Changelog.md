# Matching engine changelog

This page records notable changes to the standalone `osm-public-transport-matcher` package, its adapters, matching behaviour, result production and command-line interface. Review-application releases are recorded separately in the [review application changelog](https://github.com/openTdataCH/stop_sync_osm_atlas/blob/main/documentation/Changelog.md).

Changes to the [result bundle contract](Result%20bundle%20format.md) are recorded in both changelogs: here from the producer side and in the application changelog from the consumer side.

---

## Unreleased

### Matching

- **Identifier matching** — A new first rule, `identifier`, links every OSM element whose identifier tag names a source stop within 150 m to that stop, as `sloid_identifier`. In the Swiss profile, the tags are `ref:sloid` and `railway:ref:sloid` (an `IdentifierRule` of `profiles.SWITZERLAND`), and one tag may list several SLOIDs separated by `;`. A stop takes every element that names it, and the element's PTv2 partner follows it when the element names one platform. On the Swiss data of 6 November 2026, 799 of the 806 OSM elements whose SLOID names an ATLAS platform are matched to that platform, against 487 of 559 before. The other seven are four disused elements, one station and two elements whose SLOID was copied from a stop 200 m away: these two are reported and matched to their own stop by route evidence. See [E2.1](2.1%20Reference%20matching.md#identifier-matching-identifier).
- **Station shortcuts after identifiers** — Where an identifier linked part of a station, the reference rule's `one_osm` and `one_source` branches and the name rule's `one_osm` branch no longer apply there: the elements left are not the whole station. Only platform codes link them; the route and distance rules decide the rest. See [E2.1](2.1%20Reference%20matching.md#stations-already-touched-by-identifiers).
- **Platform labels before shared routes** — A line can call at several platforms of a big station, so the GTFS route rule leaves out candidates labelled with another platform when a candidate within 50 m has `local_ref` equal to the stop's platform code. At Zürich HB, Löwenstrasse, platform 32 no longer matches the stop position labelled 31; stops without a platform code are unaffected. See [E2.3](2.3%20GTFS%20route%20matching.md#platform-labels).
- **Year-ignored route IDs need the stops to agree** — A `gtfs:route_id` equal to a source route ID only once the timetable year is ignored now links two lines only when they have the same line number and share at least one stop. Between timetable years an ID can pass to another line, often one with the same number in another town: on the Swiss data of 6 November 2026, 29 of the 395 such links joined lines without a stop in common, such as bus 11 around Fribourg and Basel's bus 11. A refused ID no longer takes the source line, which can go to the OSM line that does run there. See [E3.2](3.2%20Route-Route%20Matching.md#by-route-identifier).
- **Lines linked by number and stops** — An OSM line still unlinked after the identifier pass is linked to the source line with the same mode and line number when they share at least four stops, one pair of their itineraries reaches the itinerary threshold (0.8), and each line leads the other's next candidate by 0.1. These links have `match_method` `line_number_and_stops`; there are 116 on the same data. See [E3.2](3.2%20Route-Route%20Matching.md#by-line-number-and-stops).
- **Swiss timetable line numbers** — `compare_routes()` takes a `line_number_alias`; the Swiss runner passes `timetable_line_number()`, so an OSM `ref` such as `31.158` compares as line `158`.
- **Itineraries compared by station** — Two stop calls are now the same stop when they are at the same platform or at the same station; before, two calls resolved to different platforms of one station never matched. A stop position and a platform listed one after the other also form one call when stop matching linked them to different platforms of the station. Between otherwise equal pairs, more shared platforms win. The S10 relation *Uetliberg → Selnau* now pairs with the source itinerary ending at Selnau, 8 of 8, instead of the one running on to Zürich HB, 8 of 9, whose Selnau call is the platform OSM's stop position is matched to. On the Swiss data of 6 November 2026, 3,728 itineraries are paired instead of 2,477: 1,269 pairs are new and 18 are gone. 89% of the new pairs start and end at the stops the relation's `from` and `to` tags name, against 7 of the 18 gone. The line fallback scores itineraries the same way and links 224 lines instead of 116; with the identifiers of the 1,660 exactly matched lines hidden, it finds 1,267 of them again instead of 974, and still one wrong line, IR 26. See [E3.2](3.2%20Route-Route%20Matching.md#step-4-pair-the-itineraries).
- **The same ends first** — Eligible itinerary pairs are now ranked by their ends first: a pair whose first and last stops are the same comes before one sharing one end, then the higher ratio, then more shared stops, then more shared platforms. Before, more shared stops came first, so bus 791 *Schwägalp → Urnäsch* paired with the source itinerary running on to St. Gallen (12 of 14 stops) rather than the one ending at Urnäsch (11 of 12). The ends rank pairs but are not required: requiring them would have refused 232 of the 3,728 pairs, 137 of which start and end where the relation's `from` and `to` tags say. On the Swiss data of 6 November 2026, 21 pairs change: 5 of the 10 new pairs agree with those tags, against 1 of the 11 replaced, and 3,727 itineraries are paired. See [E3.2](3.2%20Route-Route%20Matching.md#step-4-pair-the-itineraries).
- **Short lines linked by number and stops** — A line linked by number and stops shares four stops, or every stop of the line that has fewer, but never fewer than two; before, four were required of every line, so no cableway between two stations and few funiculars could be linked this way. With the identifiers of the 1,660 exactly matched lines hidden, the pass finds 1,297 of them again instead of 1,267, aerialways 12 of 34 instead of none, and still one wrong line, IR 26. See [E3.2](3.2%20Route-Route%20Matching.md#by-line-number-and-stops).
- **Station-only GTFS calls keep their station** — A Swiss trip without a planned platform calls at the station's SLOID, such as `ch:1:sloid:1075`. Such a call resolves to no platform, and the Swiss adapter read the station number only from a numeric stop ID prefix, which SLOID IDs lack, so 24,862 calls (19% of ferry calls, 33% of aerialway calls) had no stop identity at all. The adapter now falls back to the feed's `didok`, and a call resolved to an ATLAS platform takes that platform's station first. The GTFS-to-ATLAS cache version is now 3, so cached products are rebuilt once. On the Swiss data of 7 November 2026, together with the previous change: 2,300 lines are linked instead of 2,250, none lost, with 274 links by number and stops instead of 224; 4,405 itineraries are paired instead of 3,727; with identifiers hidden, the line pass finds 1,460 of 1,660 again, still with IR 26 as the only wrong line. Stop matches and problems are unchanged. See [E1.2](1.2%20GTFS%20ATLAS%20data.md#source_stop_callscsv) and [E3.2](3.2%20Route-Route%20Matching.md#step-3-build-itineraries-and-stop-calls).

### Inputs

- **Platform ways with a SLOID** — The Overpass query also downloads ways with `ref:sloid` or `railway:ref:sloid`, and `read_osm()` keeps those that are not stops in their own right as `identifier_only` elements. They are linked only by their identifier, after all rules, as extra elements that take no stop away from another. See [E1.3](1.3%20OSM%20data.md#stop-identifiers).

### Results and operations

- **`identifier` problem** — P1 when an element's identifier names a stop more than 150 m away, P2 when it names no stop, P3 when it is malformed, such as a UIC number in `ref:sloid`. One problem per element, with the values in its evidence. See [E4.1](4.1%20Stop%20Problems.md#identifier-problems).
- **Bundle** — `osm_nodes` rows carry `identifier_only`; a missing value counts as false, so earlier schema 1 bundles stay valid. The progress catalog has a new stage, `stop_matching.identifier`.

## Version 0.1.0

First public release.

### Matching

- **Source-neutral core** — Stop matching, problem detection, route comparison and quality metrics run on normalized records, without file, network or database access.
- **Dataset profiles** — `MatchingProfile` holds the rule order, reference rules, OSM grouping and thresholds; `profiles.SWITZERLAND` is the Swiss policy and the generic default needs no SLOID or UIC identifiers.
- **Match types name their evidence** — Each match records the rule and branch that decided it, such as `uic_reference_local_ref`, `gtfs_shared_route` or `local_ref_distance`; OSM stop units name their PTv2 layout, such as `ptv2_two_sided_stop`. See [E2](2.%20Matching%20process.md#the-matching-rules).
- **Route comparison** — Every adapter delivers `source_routes`, `source_itineraries` and `source_stop_calls`; `compare_routes()` matches line families and ordered itineraries against OSM PTv2 relations.
- **Disused OSM elements** — An element with a lifecycle-prefixed stop tag (`disused:`, `abandoned:`, `removed:`, `demolished:`, `razed:`, `destroyed:` or `was:`) is set aside before matching and returned in `MatchingOutput.disused_osm`. A source stop whose only candidate is disused is unmatched. See [E2](2.%20Matching%20process.md#rules-shared-by-all-matching-rules).
- **One OSM call per stop visit** — A PTv2 relation lists a stop's stop position and its platform one after the other. These two members form one stop call, which keeps the member resolved to a source stop, so an OSM itinerary has one call per stop like its source itinerary and the stop ratio compares like with like. Bundles carry one OSM `stop_calls` row per stop visit; `stop_sequence` follows the relation member positions. See [E3.2](3.2%20Route-Route%20Matching.md#step-3-build-itineraries-and-stop-calls).
- **`disused` problem** — P1 when an unmatched source stop shares the element's station reference, P2 when an unprefixed stop tag remains or a route relation still lists the element, none for a cleanly retired element. Disused elements get no `unmatched` problem. See [E4.1](4.1%20Stop%20Problems.md#disused-problems).

### Inputs

- **Adapters** — Generic GTFS (folder or ZIP), Swiss ATLAS with GTFS-to-SLOID resolution, and OSM XML. External datasets implement the public `SourceAdapter` contract without changes to this package.
- **Swiss acquisition** — Optional downloads with staged, verified caches; the Swiss GTFS feed is reduced to trip patterns in DuckDB.
- **Public transport routes only** — Route relations count when their `route` tag is a public transport mode (`bus`, `trolleybus`, `minibus`, `share_taxi`, `coach`, `tram`, `light_rail`, `train`, `subway`, `monorail`, `ferry`, `funicular`, `aerialway`), or when they have no `route` tag and belong to a route master of such a mode. Ski pistes, hiking, cycling and running routes and `route=railway` infrastructure relations pass through stops too, but are neither downloaded nor used as route evidence or route products. `PUBLIC_TRANSPORT_ROUTES` in `core/osm_tags.py` defines the list for the query and the parser. See [E1.3](1.3%20OSM%20data.md#which-route-relations-are-services).
- **Fully retired stops downloaded** — The Overpass query also selects elements whose every stop tag is lifecycle-prefixed, and seeds their route relations. `core/osm_tags.py` defines the stop selectors and lifecycle prefixes for both the query and the parser; the existing selector lines are unchanged. See [E1.3](1.3%20OSM%20data.md#disused-stops).
- **Validated Overpass snapshots** — An answer that does not parse as OSM XML, or that carries an Overpass `runtime error` remark inside HTTP 200, fails `osm.download` instead of being matched as a partial snapshot. HTTP 429 and 503, connection failures and timeouts are retried as well, `OVERPASS_API_URL` accepts a comma-separated list of endpoints, and the error names each endpoint's last failure without the error page. The snapshot's `osm_base` is recorded as `osm_data_at`. See [E1.3](1.3%20OSM%20data.md#the-download).
- **Named ATLAS and Swiss GTFS schema errors** — A missing required ATLAS column fails with `ATLAS CSV lacks required columns`; missing optional columns become warnings. The Swiss GTFS boundary selector rejects a feed without `original_stop_id`, `didok` or coordinates, and a selection with no stops. See [E1.1](1.1%20ATLAS%20stops.md#filters) and [E1.2](1.2%20GTFS%20ATLAS%20data.md).

### Results and operations

- **Result bundle, schema 1** — A validated, checksummed and atomically published directory of stops, matches, unmatched entities, groups, problems, routes, extensions and diagnostics, with source-neutral field names throughout.
- **Command line** — `osm-public-transport-matcher swiss` and `osm-public-transport-matcher gtfs`, with structured progress events (progress schema version 2).
- **Documentation** — Engine chapters on inputs, matching, routes, problems, operations and the bundle format, with generated diagrams.
- **Input warnings in the manifest, schema 1** — `metadata.warnings` lists input problems that weakened the run without stopping it. The field is optional and consumers ignore it until they show it. See [Result bundle format](Result%20bundle%20format.md#manifest).
- **Disused elements in the bundle, schema 1** — `osm_nodes` rows carry `lifecycle`, OSM `unmatched` rows a `disused` flag, disused elements are `single` stop units, and `metadata.summary.disused_osm` counts them. A missing `disused` counts as false, so earlier bundles stay valid. A consumer that predates the flag rejects a bundle with disused elements, because they have no `unmatched` problem; upgrade the consumer first. See [Result bundle format](Result%20bundle%20format.md#matches-and-unmatched-entities).

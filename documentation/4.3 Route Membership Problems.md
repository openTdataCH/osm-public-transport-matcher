# E4.3 Route membership problems

> [!WARNING]
> **Planned, not implemented.** This page describes the intended design. No route membership problems are emitted today.

Route membership problems would describe the relationship between a route and its stops, for routes that exist in both datasets. They answer: *does the route serve the same stops, in the same order and direction?*

## Missing member

A stop belongs to a route in one dataset but not in the other:

- **Missing in OSM:** GTFS says route X serves stop A, but the OSM node of stop A is not a member of route X's relation.
- **Missing in GTFS:** stop A is a member of route X in OSM, but the timetable does not serve it.

Example: *Zürich HB* is served by the S3 in the timetable, but its OSM platform is not part of the S3 relation.

## Sequence mismatch

The same stops, in a different order. A detection could compare the ordered stop lists of matched itineraries and measure the difference, for example as an edit distance.

Proposed priorities:

| Priority | Difference |
|---|---|
| **P1** | More than 40%, which suggests a broken relation |
| **P2** | 10% to 40% |
| **P3** | Less than 10% |

## Direction mismatch

The stop is a member on both sides, but in a different directional context:

- **Direction:** different paired itineraries or headsigns, for example *towards A* against *towards B*.
- **Member role:** direction-specific OSM roles such as entry-only or exit-only.
- **Platform:** the route uses platform 1 in GTFS but platform 2 in OSM, when the platforms are separate elements.
